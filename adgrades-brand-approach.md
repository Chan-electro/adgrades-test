# The AdGrades Brand Approach
### How a Hassan-born agency should look, feel, and behave on the internet.

*Written as a strategic brand + design brief. Not a template. Not a mood board. A decision document.*

---

## The Problem With Your Current Site

Let's be direct.

AdGrades calls itself an "anti-trend" agency. The website looks like every other agency that launched in 2023. Clean gradients, floating badges, a phone mockup, safe cards, and the cyan brand color drowned in generic layout patterns. It could be any of 40,000 Webflow agencies.

That's not a branding problem. That's a credibility problem. If the homepage says "we're different" and the design says "we used the same Framer template as everyone else," the visitor trusts the design, not the words.

The new site needs to fix one thing above all: **the design itself must prove the philosophy.** Every pixel should feel like it was chosen by people who actually think differently about marketing. Not people who say they do.

---

## Who Is This Site Actually For?

Before we touch a single color or font, let's get clear on who's landing on this site:

**Primary audience — The founder who's tired of agencies.**
A startup founder, a D2C brand owner, a local business operator in India. They've worked with 1–3 agencies before. Those agencies showed them pretty reports and delivered nothing. They're skeptical. They don't want another deck full of "synergies" and "omnichannel strategies." They want someone who talks straight, shows receipts, and actually gives a damn.

**Secondary audience — The brand manager researching options.**
They'll scan 8 agency sites in 20 minutes. They'll remember the one that felt different. Not the one with the smoothest animation — the one that made them stop scrolling because something felt unexpectedly real.

**Tertiary audience — Potential hires.**
Smart, young, creative people in Bengaluru or Hassan who want to work somewhere that doesn't feel corporate. The careers page isn't a checkbox — it's a recruitment ad that runs 24/7.

The common thread: **all three audiences are allergic to bullshit.** The site should respect that.

---

## The Brand Personality — Defined in Human Terms

Forget "modern, innovative, data-driven." Every agency on earth claims those. Here's what AdGrades should *actually* feel like:

**1. The friend who's annoyingly good at their job.**
Not a slick salesperson. Not a guru. The person in your group chat who casually drops insight that changes how you think about your business — then goes back to cracking jokes. Competent, warm, zero pretension.

**2. Indian, unapologetically.**
Hassan isn't Silicon Valley. That's a feature, not a bug. The site shouldn't cosplay as a San Francisco startup. Use the specificity of where you're from. The language should feel like talking to a smart person in Bengaluru — direct, a little irreverent, mixing English with the confidence of someone who doesn't need to prove they're "global."

**3. Obsessed with proof, not polish.**
Every agency has a "results" section. Most of them are vague. AdGrades has real numbers — ROAS 3.1x, 320 leads, 500% e-commerce growth. The design should put those numbers front and center, not buried in a carousel. Proof is the brand.

**4. Anti-trend means anti-trend in design too.**
If every agency site in 2026 has smooth scroll animations, gradient blobs, and bento grids — don't do that. The design language itself should break patterns. Not for chaos — for clarity and memorability.

## The Visual Language

### Color — Built From The Logo

The AdGrades logo is already distinctive. It's not corporate navy or safe SaaS blue — it's electric cyan-to-turquoise, a gradient that sits between technology and creativity. The interlocking "A" form with the 4-pointed sparkle is confident, geometric, and forward-leaning. The color system must amplify what the logo already communicates, not fight it.

Three logo variants exist and each tells us something about how the palette should flex:
- **Variant 1 (Icon on dark navy):** The logo is the brightest element on a deep, dark surface. This is the default web experience — dark ground, cyan as the light source.
- **Variant 2 (Full lockup, light text on black):** "Ad Grades" in cool silver-gray. The text is deliberately quieter than the icon. The typography doesn't compete with the mark.
- **Variant 3 (Stealth/dark-on-dark):** The logo nearly disappears into the background with only the cyan sparkle visible. This teaches us something powerful — the sparkle alone is enough to signal the brand. Restraint is built into the identity.

**The palette — derived from the logo, not invented beside it:**

| Role | Color | Hex | Source / Logic |
|------|-------|-----|----------------|
| Primary background | Deep space navy | `#0D1117` | Pulled from the logo's dark backdrop. Not pure black (which feels lifeless) — this has just enough blue-black warmth to feel intentional and let the cyan sing. |
| Secondary background | Elevated dark | `#161B22` | For cards, nav bars, elevated surfaces. A shade lighter than the base — creates depth without borders. |
| Primary text | Cool silver | `#C9D1D9` | Matches the "Ad Grades" wordmark in Variant 2. Cool-toned, not warm. Feels technical, not cozy. High contrast against the dark base (WCAG AA compliant). |
| Secondary text | Muted steel | `#8B949E` | For descriptions, captions, timestamps. Quiet but legible. |
| Brand cyan — Primary accent | Electric cyan | `#00D1FF` | The hero color. Sampled from the brighter end of the logo gradient. Used for CTAs, links, hover states, the sparkle element, and any moment where the brand needs to "light up." |
| Brand cyan — Deep | Anchor teal | `#0A8CC2` | The darker end of the logo gradient. For pressed/active states, secondary buttons, borders, and anywhere you need depth without leaving the brand family. |
| Accent — Warm contrast | Signal coral | `#FF6B4A` | Cyan's complementary opposite on the color wheel. Used sparingly — for alerts, key metric highlights, "hot" data points on the Signal Board, and hover micro-accents. Never as a background. This is the 5% color that creates visual electricity against the cool palette. |
| Accent — Warm secondary | Soft amber | `#FFAA33` | For star ratings, badges, and secondary warm moments. Pairs with coral but softer. |
| Surface / Neutral | Charcoal gray | `#21262D` | Dividers, input fields, card borders. Dark enough to blend, light enough to separate. |
| Success | Teal green | `#2EA88A` | Functional only. Form success, positive metrics. Stays in the cool family. |
| Error | Muted red | `#E85D4A` | Functional only. Form errors, negative metrics. |

**The 60-30-10 rule in practice:**
- **60% — Dark backgrounds** (`#0D1117` + `#161B22`). The site is dark-first. This isn't a trend — it's the logo's native environment. The dark variants of the logo prove the brand was designed to live here.
- **30% — Cool text and neutral surfaces** (`#C9D1D9` + `#8B949E` + `#21262D`). Text, cards, structure. The silver-gray tone of the wordmark carries through all body copy.
- **10% — Electric cyan** (`#00D1FF`). This is the spark. CTAs, links, hover states, highlighted numbers, the logo sparkle animation. When cyan appears, it means "pay attention here." If it's everywhere, it means nothing.

**Cyan gradient — where to use it and where not to:**
The logo uses a cyan-to-teal gradient. On the website, use it ONLY for:
- The logo itself
- One hero moment per page (a gradient line, a glowing edge, a subtle background wash behind a key stat)
- Hover states on primary CTAs (solid cyan → gradient on hover)

Do NOT gradient-wash entire sections, backgrounds, or cards. The gradient is the logo's signature. Diluting it across the site turns a brand mark into wallpaper.

**The coral accent — why it matters:**
A site that's all cyan-on-dark will feel monotone and cold. One warm accent color creates visual tension — the eye jumps between cool and warm, which keeps attention. Coral (`#FF6B4A`) is cyan's natural complement. Use it like a highlighter pen: on the one number that matters most in a case study, on a "new" badge, on the notification dot. Maybe 20 instances across the entire site. That's enough.

**Light mode (optional but recommended):**
If you build a light mode toggle, flip the hierarchy:

| Role | Light Mode Hex |
|------|---------------|
| Background | `#F6F8FA` (cool off-white) |
| Text | `#1A1F2B` (near the logo dark) |
| Brand cyan | `#0A8CC2` (use the deeper teal — electric cyan washes out on white) |
| Cards | `#FFFFFF` with `#E1E4E8` borders |

The logo's Variant 3 (dark-on-dark) is essentially already a light-mode-ready asset when inverted. The mark works across both contexts.

**What to avoid:**
- Pure black (`#000000`) backgrounds — lifeless, no depth
- Purple or violet accents — reads as "AI startup" or "crypto" in 2026
- Blue-to-purple gradients — the single most overused pattern in Indian agency/tech sites
- Using cyan at full saturation on large surfaces — it becomes eye-strain. Reserve full brightness for small, intentional moments
- Warm-toned backgrounds — the logo's DNA is cool. Warm backgrounds will make it feel like a guest on someone else's site

### Logo Usage on the Website

The three variants you have each serve a specific context:

| Variant | Where to use it |
|---------|----------------|
| **Icon only (cyan on dark)** | Favicon, mobile nav collapsed state, loading placeholder, og:image fallback, the single sparkle in the footer |
| **Full lockup (cyan icon + silver text on dark)** | Desktop navigation header, about page hero, footer (at larger breakpoints) |
| **Stealth variant (dark-on-dark with cyan sparkle)** | Background watermark on case study pages, hover-reveal on portfolio cards, subtle texture on the 404 page |

**The sparkle element deserves its own life.** Extract the 4-pointed star from the logo and use it as a recurring micro-brand element:
- As a bullet point replacement in key lists (instead of dots or checkmarks)
- As a subtle animation on page load — a single soft pulse
- As the cursor trail accent on portfolio pages
- As a section divider between major content blocks (centered, small, in `#0A8CC2`)

This gives the brand a "signature gesture" beyond the full logo — the way Nike's swoosh can exist without the wordmark.

**Logo animation (homepage only):** On first load, the sparkle pulses once from `#0A8CC2` to `#00D1FF` and back. Subtle, 600ms, ease-in-out. After that, it's static. The animation signals life without being distracting. It says "we're here" once and then gets out of the way.

### Typography

This is where the site lives or dies. Typography IS the brand on the web.

**Recommended font pairing:**

| Role | Font | Why |
|------|------|-----|
| Display / H1 | **Space Grotesk** (or Clash Display) | Geometric but with personality. Not boring like Inter. Not try-hard like a custom serif. The slightly squared letterforms feel technical and grounded — like an agency that actually builds things. |
| Body | **General Sans** (or Satoshi) | Clean, highly readable, designed for long-form. Works at 16px on mobile without squinting. |
| Mono / Data | **JetBrains Mono** (or IBM Plex Mono) | For stats, metrics, code-like elements, the Signal Board. Signals precision and technical rigor. |

**Typography rules:**

- H1 headlines should be massive. 80px+ on desktop. Not because "big text is trendy" — because confidence takes up space. An anti-trend agency shouldn't whisper.
- Body text at 18px minimum. If you're asking people to read, respect their eyes.
- Letter-spacing on headlines: tight. –0.02em to –0.04em. Compressed type feels intentional and editorial.
- ALL CAPS only for tiny labels (12px). Never for headings — it reads as shouting, not confidence.
- Mix weights dramatically: 800 weight headlines next to 400 weight body. The contrast creates visual rhythm without decoration.

### Texture and Surface

This is what separates a site that looks real from one that looks AI-generated.

**Grain overlay.** Subtle. 3–5% opacity. Applied to hero backgrounds and dark sections. This single technique makes flat digital surfaces feel like printed paper. It adds warmth, tactility, and a subtle analog quality that says "a human made this, not a template."

How to implement:
```css
.grain::after {
  content: '';
  position: fixed;
  top: -50%; left: -50%;
  width: 200%; height: 200%;
  background-image: url('/noise.svg');
  opacity: 0.035;
  pointer-events: none;
  z-index: 9999;
}
```

**No gradients on backgrounds.** Flat darks, flat lights. Gradients are the default output of every AI design tool. Flat color with grain > gradient without it.

**Photography style:** If using team photos or client work, shoot with natural light, slight overexposure, slightly desaturated. Think documentary, not stock. If budget doesn't allow a shoot, use no photos rather than bad ones. Illustration or type-only sections are more honest than Unsplash filler.

### Layout Philosophy

**Rule: Break one thing per section.**

The site shouldn't be complete chaos — that's just bad UX. But each section should have one moment where the layout does something unexpected. One asymmetric element. One oversized number. One piece of text that runs vertically. One image that bleeds off the edge.

This gives the viewer constant micro-surprises without destroying usability.

**Specific layout principles:**

**1. The offset grid.**
Instead of perfect 12-column centering, offset content blocks by 1–2 columns. Text starts at column 2 instead of column 1. Images bleed into column 11 instead of stopping at 10. This creates tension and visual interest without requiring any special components.

**2. Sticky context + scrolling content.**
Pin a section label or stat on the left while related content scrolls on the right. This is how editorial publications handle long-form content. It gives the user orientation without a traditional sidebar.

**3. Full-width type breaks.**
Between sections, use a single massive line of text (120px+) that runs full-width. No card, no container. Just type on the page. These act as breathing moments and chapter markers. Example: a thin sans-serif line that says "We built brands for 25+ businesses. Not likes. Brands." spanning the full viewport.

**4. Bento grid — but only for the Signal Board.**
Bento grids are effective for dense data display. Use it for the portfolio/Signal Board page where you're showing multiple client metrics. Don't use it on the homepage — on the homepage, the layout should feel editorial, not dashboard-like.

---

### Motion and Interaction

**The rule: motion should reveal, not decorate.**

Bad animation: text flies in from the left for no reason.
Good animation: text appears by "unmasking" as you scroll, like you're peeling back a layer.

**Specific motion recommendations:**

| Element | Motion | Technique |
|---------|--------|-----------|
| Headlines | Clip-path reveal on scroll | Text is masked and reveals line-by-line as you scroll into the section. Feels like the page is being printed in real time. |
| Stats/numbers | Count-up triggered at 60% viewport entry | The numbers should count up FAST (400ms), not slow. Slow count-ups feel like 2018. |
| Client logos | No marquee. Static grid, subtle opacity shift on hover. | The infinite scroll marquee is on every agency site. A static grid where logos subtly brighten on hover is quieter and classier. |
| Page transitions | Crossfade with slight vertical offset (20px) | Smooth, fast (300ms), minimal. No page wipes, no loader screens. |
| Cursor | Custom cursor only on portfolio/case study pages | A small dot cursor with a trailing circle that expands over clickable elements. Only on pages where exploration is the goal — not sitewide. |
| Scroll | Native smooth scroll. No hijacked scroll. | Scroll-jacking is hostile UX. Let people scroll at their own speed. |

**What to never do:**
- Parallax backgrounds (reads as 2016 immediately)
- Loading screens with the logo (pretentious for a 15-person agency)
- Hover effects on every element (visual noise)
- Lottie animations on the hero (screams "we downloaded this from LottieFiles")

## The Copy Voice

### The Anti-Agency-Speak Mandate

Every sentence on the site should pass this test: **"Would a real person actually say this out loud?"**

If the answer is no, rewrite it.

**Kill list — phrases that should never appear on the site:**

| Instead of this | Write this |
|----------------|------------|
| "Comprehensive digital solutions" | "We do marketing, design, and development. All of it." |
| "Transform your digital presence" | "Make your brand impossible to ignore." |
| "Data-driven strategies" | "We look at what's working and do more of it." |
| "Leverage cutting-edge technology" | "We use good tools." |
| "Synergize your brand touchpoints" | (Just delete this entirely) |
| "End-to-end solutions" | "From the logo to the last ad — we handle it." |
| "Our team of experts" | "15 people who are unreasonably good at this." |
| "We're passionate about..." | (Show it. Don't say it.) |

### Voice Characteristics

**Short sentences.** Long sentences are where meaning goes to hide. The best copy on the site should average 8–12 words per sentence.

**Specific over vague.** "We grew MK Streetwear's e-commerce revenue by 500% in 6 months" > "We deliver exceptional results."

**Confident, not arrogant.** There's a difference between "We're the best agency in India" (arrogant, unprovable) and "We've scaled 25 brands in 3 years. Here's the proof." (confident, verifiable).

**Humor, occasionally.** Not jokes. Just honesty delivered with enough self-awareness to make the reader smile. Example in a team bio: "Chandu speaks fluent AI, data, and caffeine — often all at once." That's already on the current site and it's one of the best lines. More of this energy.

## Homepage — Section-by-Section Approach

Here's how the homepage should flow, with the emotional intention of each section:

### 1. Hero — "Stop them."
No label. No "CREATIVE MARKETING AGENCY" tag. The hero should be one line:

> **"We don't chase trends. We build brands that outlast them."**

That's it. One line, massive type, dark background, the word "outlast" lit up in brand cyan (`#00D1FF`). Below it: a single CTA — "See the proof" (scrolls to the Signal Board section or links to /portfolio). No secondary CTA. No paragraph of body copy. No phone mockup. The AdGrades sparkle icon animates subtly in the corner — a single pulse, then still. That's the only motion on first load.

The hero's job is to make someone think "this doesn't look like every other agency" and keep scrolling. One clear thought does that. A wall of text doesn't.

### 2. Proof Bar — "Back it up immediately."
Right after the hero. No section title. Just raw numbers.

```
25+ brands scaled  ·  ₹2Cr+ in ad spend managed  ·  3.1x avg ROAS  ·  90% client retention
```

JetBrains Mono. Small (14px). Cool silver text. The numbers themselves in brand cyan. No icons. No animations. The understatement is the point — the numbers are strong enough to speak without decoration. A single 1px line in `#21262D` separates this from the hero above.

### 3. Anti-Trend Manifesto — "Say what you believe."
This is the section most agency sites don't have. A 4–6 sentence declaration of what "anti-trend" means. Written in first person ("We"). Not a mission statement — a stance.

Something like:

> "Most agencies sell you what's trending. We think that's lazy. Trends expire. Brands compound. We build marketing systems that get stronger over time — not campaigns that need to be replaced every quarter. That's what anti-trend means."

This section should feel like an op-ed, not a brochure. It should make people who agree nod, and people who don't self-select out. That's good positioning.

### 4. Signal Board Preview — "Show, don't tell."
Pull 4–6 live client results from the Signal Board. Real names, real metrics, real timelines.

| Client | What we did | The number |
|--------|------------|------------|
| Delhi 65 | Ads + Social | ROAS 3.1x |
| Medhavi Classes | Lead Gen + Content | 320 leads/month |
| MK Streetwear | Brand Build + E-com | +500% transactions |
| Dew Drop Homestay | Full Digital | +300% direct bookings |

Each one links to a deeper case study. The section CTA: "Explore all 25+ client stories →"

No carousel. A clean grid. Each card shows the client name, what you did, and one killer number. That's it.

### 5. Services — "Here's what we actually do."
Not 6 cards with emoji icons. Instead: a single-column text list.

```
Social Media Management          from ₹12,999/mo
Performance Marketing            from ₹18,000/mo
Web Development                  from ₹19,999 build
Brand Design & Identity          Custom scope
Video Production                 from ₹1,500/reel
Email + Content Marketing        from ₹3,999/mo
E-commerce Builds               from ₹74,999 build
Mobile Apps                      from ₹39,999
CRM / ERP Systems               from ₹49,999
```

Why show pricing on the homepage? Because transparency is the anti-trend move. Every other agency hides pricing behind a "contact us" wall. Showing it says "we're not afraid of the conversation." It also filters out bad-fit leads before they waste your team's time.

Each service name is a link to the full service page. Service names in cool silver, prices in brand cyan. On hover, the entire row gets a subtle `#161B22` background shift and the service name transitions to cyan. Clean. Scannable. Useful.

### 6. How We Work — "Demystify the process."
Four steps. But don't present them as a cute numbered timeline. Present them as honest descriptions:

> **Week 1:** We shut up and listen. We learn your business, your customers, your numbers, and what's failed before.
>
> **Weeks 2–3:** We build a strategy. Not a 40-slide deck — a focused plan with specific actions, timelines, and expected outcomes.
>
> **Months 1–3:** We execute. Ads go live. Content ships. The website launches. You see reports that show real numbers, not vanity metrics.
>
> **Month 4+:** We optimize. What's working gets more budget. What's not gets killed. No ego, no "let's give it more time." Just data.

This is dramatically more useful than "Discovery → Strategy → Execution → Optimization" with generic descriptions.

### 7. Testimonials — "Let them say it."
Pick 3 testimonials. Not 14. Three. The strongest ones.

Display them large. Full-width. One at a time. No carousel — just three stacked blocks, each with:
- The quote (shortened to 2 sentences max)
- Name and company
- One specific result

If the visitor wants more, link to the /clients page.

### 8. Final CTA — "Make the ask."
Darkest section on the page (`#0A0E13`). A thin cyan gradient line at the top edge — the only decorative gradient on the entire homepage. One line:

> **"Let's see if we're a fit."**

Two buttons side by side:
- "Book a free strategy call" → Calendly (solid cyan background `#00D1FF`, dark text)
- "WhatsApp us" → wa.me link (outlined, cyan border, no fill)

That's it. No "join hundreds of businesses" puffery. Just a direct, confident invitation. The cyan CTA button is the single brightest element on the page at this point — after a full page of restraint, it pulls focus exactly where it should.

### 9. Footer — "Be useful."
Four columns on `#0A0E13`. Contact info, service links, social links, legal links. The AdGrades sparkle icon (just the sparkle, not the full logo) sits quietly at the top of the footer in `#0A8CC2`. A small line at the bottom in muted steel: "Built in Hassan. Working across India. © 2026 AdGrades."

The "Built in Hassan" is intentional. It's specific. It's real. It's the opposite of agencies that pretend to be everywhere and nowhere.

## Key Inner Pages — Approach Notes

### /services
Each service gets its own page. Not a card — a page. With pricing tiers, what's included, what's not, timeline, and a CTA. The bundle packages live at the bottom of /services as a separate section.

### /clients (Case Studies)
This is the most important page after the homepage. Each client should be a mini-story: the problem, what AdGrades did, the results, and a quote. Keep each case study to one screen (no scrolling required). Dense, visual, metric-rich.

### /signal-board (Portfolio)
Keep the interactive timeline scrubber — it's genuinely unique and no competitor has it. But redesign the visual presentation to match the new brand language. Deep space navy background (`#0D1117`), monospaced metrics in cool silver, key numbers highlighted in brand cyan, and the single most impressive metric per client punched up in signal coral (`#FF6B4A`) — the warm accent that makes one data point impossible to ignore.

### /about
Lead with the manifesto, not the team. The story of why AdGrades exists comes before who works there. The team section should have real, good photos — or illustrated avatars if you can't do a proper shoot. The witty bios you already have are excellent. Keep them.

### /careers
Make this page feel like a recruitment campaign, not an HR listing. Show the culture. Show the actual office (or remote setup). List benefits first, roles second. The certificate verification feature is a nice unique touch — keep it but don't lead with it.

### /contact
The current 6-field form is fine. But add a WhatsApp button that's equally prominent. For Indian businesses, WhatsApp IS the contact form. Don't fight that.

## Frontend Technical Direction

### Stack Recommendation
| Layer | Tool | Rationale |
|-------|------|-----------|
| Framework | **Next.js 14+ (App Router)** | SSR for SEO, server components for speed, image optimization built in. |
| Styling | **Tailwind CSS** | Utility-first = fast iteration. No custom CSS files to maintain. |
| Animation | **Framer Motion** | Best React animation library. Handles scroll-triggered reveals, layout animations, page transitions. |
| CMS | **Sanity.io** (or Contentlayer for markdown) | Team needs to update testimonials, case studies, job listings without touching code. |
| Hosting | **Vercel** | Zero-config deploys from Git. Edge network = fast in India. |
| Forms | **Formspree** or **Resend** | Simple, reliable, no backend needed. |
| Analytics | **Plausible** (or PostHog) | Privacy-friendly. Lightweight. No cookie banner needed. |
| Fonts | Self-hosted via `next/font` | No Google Fonts waterfall. Fonts load with the page, not after. |

### Performance Targets
- Lighthouse score: 95+ on mobile
- First Contentful Paint: < 1.2s
- Largest Contentful Paint: < 2.5s
- Total page weight: < 500KB (excluding images)
- No layout shift (CLS < 0.1)

These aren't aspirational — they're requirements. A slow agency website is an agency that doesn't practice what it preaches.

### Accessibility
- Full keyboard navigation
- Proper heading hierarchy (h1 → h2 → h3, no skips)
- Alt text on every image
- Color contrast ratio 4.5:1 minimum (the cyan-on-dark combo passes at `#00D1FF` on `#0D1117` — verify the coral accent and silver text too)
- Reduced motion media query respected
- Focus indicators visible

## What Makes This Not "AI Slop"

Let's address this directly, because it matters.

AI-generated agency sites have a specific look. You can spot them in 2 seconds:

- Perfectly symmetrical layouts with no visual tension
- Generic gradient blobs (purple-to-blue is the worst offender)
- Floating 3D objects that have nothing to do with the brand
- Hero sections with 3+ lines of copy, all saying nothing
- Stock illustrations in the "flat outline" style
- A phone mockup showing a fake app interface
- The word "solutions" used more than once
- Brand colors sprayed everywhere with no restraint — if the accent color appears 200 times, it stops being an accent
- Cookie-cutter Framer/Webflow template bones visible underneath

**How AdGrades avoids all of this:**

1. **Asymmetry.** AI outputs default to centered, symmetrical layouts. Offset your grids. Let things be slightly uncomfortable.

2. **Real content.** AI slop has placeholder-quality copy. "Comprehensive solutions for your digital needs." AdGrades has real client names, real numbers, real stories. Use them everywhere.

3. **Restraint.** AI design adds more. More colors, more sections, more animations. The anti-slop move is to remove. The brand cyan should appear in maybe 10% of the visible surface area — not painted across every section. When that electric `#00D1FF` shows up, it should feel like a signal flare, not background noise.

4. **Texture.** AI produces flat, clean, frictionless surfaces. Add grain. Use slightly irregular spacing. Let borders be 1px `#21262D`, not 0px or 2px. These micro-decisions add up to something that feels touched by human hands.

5. **Opinions.** AI slop is neutral. It doesn't take a stance. The AdGrades site should have a point of view baked into every section. The manifesto. The pricing transparency. The "we shut up and listen" process description. These are opinions. AI doesn't write opinions — it writes safe averages.

6. **Imperfection.** Leave one thing slightly "wrong" on purpose. A headline that's 2px larger than the grid suggests. A section with more whitespace than it "should" have. Perfection is the enemy of personality.

7. **Color with logic.** AI slop applies the brand color evenly. A human designer knows when to deploy the coral accent on exactly ONE metric in a case study — the one that matters most — while everything else stays cool silver. That single warm dot against a sea of cool tones creates a focal point that no template would produce.

---

## The One Rule

Every design decision, every line of copy, every animation on this site should answer one question:

**"Does this prove we're an anti-trend agency, or does it just say we are?"**

If the design looks like it could belong to any agency on the internet, it fails. If the copy sounds like it was pulled from a marketing textbook, it fails. If the animation was added because "other sites have it," it fails.

The site should be the best case study in AdGrades' portfolio. Because if you can't brand yourself, why would a client trust you to brand them?
